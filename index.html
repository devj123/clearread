Roughly 15–20% of people show some symptoms of dyslexia, and only a fraction ever get a formal diagnosis or accommodations (International Dyslexia Association). Reading friction also shows up for ADHD readers who lose their place, for tired eyes at the end of a long day, and for anyone reading on a phone in bad light. Most of the fixes people reach for — a "dyslexia font," bolded first letters, colored overlays — have thin or mixed evidence behind them individually. What the research supports more consistently is wider letter and word spacing (Zorzi et al., 2012, PNAS) and simply giving readers control to find what works for their own eyes. ClearRead is built around that: every control is optional, off by default where the evidence is weak, and reversible in one click. See the README for full citations and for what this tool deliberately does not claim.
