# Feedback Loop (Bounty 2)

Status: not yet collected. This file is the request template and response plan structure, ready to fill in once real reviewers have actually used the tool. It is intentionally left with the reviewer sections blank rather than filled with placeholder or invented feedback: the bounty rubric scores the depth of real feedback and the honesty of the response to it, and fabricated quotes would defeat the entire point of the exercise.

Deadline: 23:59 UTC-5 on August 15th (today). Submissions after that receive zero points for this bounty regardless of quality.

## What to ask reviewers to do

Send this (or something close to it) to three people who'd plausibly use a reading tool: a student who reads a lot for school, someone who's mentioned finding long text tiring or hard to track, a teacher or tutor, anyone who's ever mentioned a diagnosed or suspected reading difference.

> Can you try something for about five minutes? It's a small web tool called ClearRead that reformats text you paste in: different fonts, spacing, colors, syllable breaks, and a read-aloud mode. Open `demo/clearread-standalone.html` (or the link I sent), paste in something you'd normally read (an assignment, an article, whatever), and try a few of the settings. I want to know specifically: what did you actually try, what worked, and what frustrated you? "It's fine" doesn't help me as much as "I turned on the dark theme and the sliders were hard to tell apart" does.

## Response plan

Once the three logs above are filled in, write the actual plan here: what changes first, what's deferred, and what you're deliberately not changing and why. The bounty rubric rewards priorities that are argued for, not a promise to fix everything. A good response plan says things like "fixing X first because two of three reviewers hit it independently" and "not changing Y because it's a deliberate tradeoff, here's why," not just a list of every complaint with a checkmark next to it.
