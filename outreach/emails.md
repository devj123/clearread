# Outreach emails

One generic template that works for any resource room, tutor, or org, and three versions aimed specifically at Redmond/Sammamish, Washington, using real contacts. Swap the bracketed placeholders before sending. Keep each email as short as it looks here; a teacher or a nonprofit contact reading this on their phone between meetings is not going to read six paragraphs from someone they don't know.

A few notes before the templates:

- I could not find a named special-education contact at Eastlake High School specifically, so I'm not inventing one. Since you're an enrolled student there, the honest and faster path is asking your own case manager or counselor directly, not cold-emailing a staff directory. The Lake Washington School District's district-wide 504 Coordinator contact is real and verified (`section504@lwsd.org`, 425-936-1407); it's included below as a formal backup, not the first move.
- WABIDA and LDAWA are the actual Washington-state branches of IDA and LDA, so reaching out to them satisfies both "state-specific" and "the national orgs" at once.
- Every email below asks for something small: read this, try it, tell me what's wrong with it. Nobody with a full inbox responds to "please partner with my startup." A specific, low-effort ask from a student building something real gets read.

## 1. Generic: teacher resource room or tutor

**Subject:** A free reading/focus tool I built, looking for feedback from someone who actually works with students on this

Hi [Name],

I'm [Your name], a high school student in Washington. It adjusts font, spacing, and layout for easier reading, and there's a companion browser extension called Cognitive Mode that helps with getting back on task after a distraction, breaking assignments into concrete steps, and reading dense pages in a calmer layout.

I'm not selling anything, there's no account, no data collection, nothing to buy. I'm looking for a small number of students who work with you to try it for a week or two and tell me honestly what's useful and what isn't. I'd rather hear "this part didn't help" than nothing at all.
